Expertise 02 · Systemic Risk and Resilience

How far a disruption travels, and what it is worth to contain it.

Risk registers score threats one at a time. They cannot show whether a disruption stays local or propagates through the network's dependencies, inventories and lead times into a much larger loss. Systemic risk analysis quantifies that propagation, and prices the flexibility that contains it.

When this approach is needed

Typical business situations
Case 1

Exposure to chokepoints and concentrated sourcing

A large share of volume depends on one strait, port, region or supplier.

DecisionWhich sites run short first, how long can each hold out, and what does a closure cost per week?

Case 2

Sizing buffers and flexibility

The business must decide how much safety stock, second-source capacity, air-freight cover or multi-gateway contracting to pay for every year.

DecisionWhich options pay for themselves against the expected annual loss — and which do not?

Case 3

Recovery slower than survival

Some sites can only hold out for days, while recovery takes weeks.

DecisionWhere does time-to-recover exceed time-to-survive, and what closes the gap?

Case 4

Resilience reporting to the board

Leadership needs a defensible, quantified view of resilience rather than a heat map.

DecisionWhat is the expected loss under plausible disruptions, and how does each mitigation change it?

What you receive
  • A propagation model of the network, mapped to real sites, lanes and inventories
  • Time-to-survive and time-to-recover for each site under each scenario
  • Expected loss by scenario, from historical, live and hypothetical disruptions
  • A ranked portfolio of mitigation options, with annual cost and expected benefit
How an engagement runs
  1. MapCapture the network, its inventories, capacities and lead times, and the disruptions that matter.
  2. ModelRepresent how a disruption propagates — feedback, delays and capacity limits on the real topology.
  3. StressRun historical, live and hypothetical scenarios, and find the thresholds beyond which losses escalate.
  4. DecideValue the flexibility options against expected loss and agree on the portfolio to hold.
Reference
Core question
Does this disruption stay local, or does the network's structure amplify it — and where is the threshold beyond which containment fails?
Method
Propagation modelling (feedback, delay and capacity limits) on the real network topology, scenario analysis with live and historical data, and flexibility options valued against expected loss.
Evidence
The Global Disruption Atlas turns live chokepoint transit data into scenarios, re-plans the network around them, scores each site on time-to-survive versus time-to-recover, and values flexibility options against expected annual loss. It accompanies Engineering Flexibility in Supply Chain Design. The Network Stress Test adds N-1 and Monte Carlo analysis.
