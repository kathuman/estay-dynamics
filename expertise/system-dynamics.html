Expertise 01 · System Dynamics

Recurring operational problems, diagnosed at the level of structure.

When the same failure returns after every fix — stock-outs and excess inventory at the same time, recurring backlogs, overtime spikes, service collapses at peak — the cause is usually the structure of delays, feedback and capacity limits in the system, not a single decision. System dynamics identifies that structure and tests remedies before they are implemented.

When this approach is needed

Typical business situations
Case 1

Demand amplification across a supply chain

Orders swing far more upstream than end-customer demand, so the business pays for overtime, excess inventory and missed service at once.

DecisionWhich delays and ordering policies drive the amplification, and which change dampens it?

Case 2

Capacity that lags demand

A step in demand produces overshoot in inventory, hiring or backlog that planning reads as forecasting error.

DecisionHow much of the overshoot is structural, and how should replenishment and hiring policies be set?

Case 3

Policies with delayed side-effects

Deferred maintenance, aggressive inventory cuts or promotional pricing improve this quarter and erode performance later.

DecisionWhat are the long-run consequences of a policy that looks right in the short term?

Case 4

A contested problem across functions

Operations, finance and commercial teams each attribute the problem to someone else, and fixes stall.

DecisionWhat shared, testable model of the problem can the leadership team agree on and act on?

What you receive
  • A causal map of the problem, built and agreed with the people who own it
  • A calibrated stock-and-flow simulation model, with assumptions documented
  • Policy tests comparing interventions on cost, service and stability over time
  • An executive workshop and a written recommendation
How an engagement runs
  1. ScopeDefine the problem behaviour over time from historical data — the reference mode the model must explain.
  2. MapBuild the causal structure with the team through facilitated group model building.
  3. ModelQuantify it as a stock-and-flow model and calibrate it against the reference mode.
  4. TestCompare interventions and agree on the policy changes to implement.
Reference
Core question
Which structure — rather than which event — produces this recurring pattern, and which change to that structure removes it?
Method
Causal loop diagrams, stock-and-flow simulation calibrated to historical data, and group model building, in the Forrester–Sterman tradition.
Evidence
The Dynamic Cyber-Risk Model (DDRA) is a five-stock system dynamics model running in the browser; PharmaSim demonstrates the bullwhip effect in a pharmaceutical chain; the case studies include a hospital replenishment model.
