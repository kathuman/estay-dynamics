Pricing geographic concentration risk into the sourcing decision.

Sourcing-network flexibility under geographic concentration risk

Context
A research vignette developed to test a network-optimization formulation against a sourcing network with high geographic concentration in a small number of supplier regions.
Problem
Standard least-cost sourcing models treated geographic concentration as a constraint to satisfy, not a cost to price — so genuinely resilient alternatives never surfaced as competitive.
Approach
A two-stage stochastic mixed-integer formulation that priced disruption exposure directly into the sourcing decision, alongside a structural-flexibility index scoring each candidate network configuration.
Direction of outcome
Demonstrated that modest cost premiums for geographic diversification were, in expectation, cheaper than the concentration risk they replaced — a result consistent with the wider academic literature this work builds on.

This narrative is a composite written to illustrate a way of thinking, not a verbatim account of a specific engagement. Figures are directional, not disclosed client data.
