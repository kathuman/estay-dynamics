Diagnosing order amplification in a regional distribution network

Context
A multi-tier regional distribution network moving fast-moving consumer goods from central production through distributors to retail.
Problem
Order variability rose sharply at each tier moving upstream — the classic bullwhip signature — and was being read internally as a forecasting failure rather than a structural one.
Approach
Causal loop mapping with the regional planning team, paired with a live simulation exercise, to separate what was genuinely demand-driven from what was an artifact of order-batching and lead-time perception.
Direction of outcome
Shifted the fix from “get a better forecast” to “shorten the information delay and change the order policy” — cheaper, faster, and aimed at the actual cause.

This narrative is a composite written to illustrate a way of thinking, not a verbatim account of a specific engagement. Figures are directional, not disclosed client data.
